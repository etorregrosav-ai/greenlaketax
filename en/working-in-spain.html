Employment taxation

Your tax obligations while living and working in Spain

Whether you're an employee or self-employed, we help you understand and meet your tax obligations in Spain.

Income tax for employees IRPF

Personal Income Tax 19–54%

Progressive rates combining a state scale and a regional one: the top marginal rate ranges from ≈45% in Madrid up to 54% in the Valencian Community, depending on residence. Annual tax filings are required when income surpasses specified thresholds. The Spanish tax year runs January through December.

Social Security Contributions

Employee contribution ≈6.4%

Of gross salary, for pensions, healthcare and unemployment, including the worker's share of the Intergenerational Equity Mechanism (MEI).

Employer contribution ≈30.5%

Paid by employers on behalf of workers. The MEI, in force since 2023, rises gradually each year (0.9% in 2026, split between employer and employee).

Special expatriate regime Newcomers

Flat rate 24%

Newly arrived workers may qualify for a special flat-rate regime, taxed at 24% on Spanish-source income up to €600,000, for a period of six years. See the Beckham Law →

Self-employed (autónomos) Independent work

Quarterly income tax payments

Self-employed individuals face the same progressive rate structure as employees, via quarterly payments.

VAT (IVA) 21%

Quarterly VAT returns filed using form 303, at the standard rate.

Social Security contribution ≈€205–607/month

Since 2023, self-employed contributions are set across 15 real net-income brackets rather than a flat fee: the minimum monthly payment ranges from ≈€205 (lower income) to ≈€607 (higher income). Newly self-employed workers can access a flat €80/month rate during their first year.

When are you a tax resident? Residency

The 183-day rule

Individuals become tax residents after spending more than 183 days annually in Spain, or when their main economic interests are based in Spain.
